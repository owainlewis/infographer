Build Software That Doesn't Break

Downtime is expensive

Meta's 2024 outage cost nearly $100M in revenue. A one-hour Amazon outage cost an estimated $34M in sales.

per hour
of downtime
01 01 Timeout

Every outbound call needs a deadline. Without one, a slow dependency holds your threads hostage until the whole service dies.

Defense
02 02 Exponential Backoff

Wait 1s, 2s, 4s, 8s between retries. Add jitter — without it, clients retry in sync and create thundering herds.

Defense
03 03 Circuit Breaker

Track failure rate. Cross a threshold? All calls fail instantly. Three states: closed → open → half-open.

Defense
04 04 Bulkhead

Give each dependency its own resource pool. Like watertight ship compartments — one floods, the rest stay dry.

Defense
05 05 Fallback

When the primary path fails, return cached data, a default, or a simplified result. Degraded beats broken.

Recovery
06 06 Safe Retries

Use idempotency keys so duplicates produce the same result. Without this, a retried payment charges twice.

Recovery
07 07 Health Checks

Expose liveness (restart me) and readiness (stop sending traffic) endpoints. Kubernetes uses both natively.

Operations
08 08 Dead Letter Queue

Messages that always fail go to a DLQ after exhausting retries. Quarantines poison pills so healthy messages flow.

Recovery
09 09 Rate Limiting

Cap requests per client per window. Return HTTP 429 with Retry-After. Protects you as a provider, not a caller.

Defense
10 10 Observability

Three pillars: logs (what), metrics (how much), traces (where). Without all three, you're guessing.

Operations
11 11 Graceful Shutdown

On SIGTERM: stop accepting connections, finish in-flight work, exit. Every deploy is a shutdown — do it cleanly.

Operations
12 12 Load Shedding

When overwhelmed, drop low-priority requests to keep serving critical ones. Better to reject 10% than fail 100%.

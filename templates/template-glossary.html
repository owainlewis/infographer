12 Software Engineering Concepts

Every Developer Should Know
01 Idempotency

Running the same operation multiple times produces the same result. Critical for payment systems and retries.

02 CAP Theorem

You can only guarantee two of three: consistency, availability, partition tolerance. Every distributed system makes this tradeoff.

03 Eventual Consistency

Data will be consistent eventually, just not right now. The tradeoff most scalable systems accept.

04 Sharding

Splitting data across multiple databases by a key. Horizontal scaling for when one database isn't enough.

05 Circuit Breaker

Stops calling a service that's failing. Prevents cascade failures from taking down everything.

06 Backpressure

When a system pushes back against incoming load it can't handle. The queue is your natural pressure valve.

07 CQRS

Separate read and write models. Optimize each side independently for different access patterns.

08 Event Sourcing

Store events, not state. Replay the event log to reconstruct any point in time.

09 Saga Pattern

Coordinate distributed transactions without a central coordinator. Each step has a compensating rollback.

10 Blue-Green Deploy

Run two identical environments. Switch traffic instantly between them. Zero-downtime releases.

11 Feature Flags

Toggle functionality without deploying. Ship code dark, enable for 1% of users, then roll out.

12 Observability

Logs, metrics, traces. The three pillars that tell you what's happening inside your system.

You don't need all 12 on day one

Start with idempotency, circuit breakers, and observability. These three cover 80% of production failures. Add the rest as your system grows and the pain points emerge.
